# Character Bible

Do not begin with personality labels. Begin with position, desire, wound, leverage, secret, and action pattern.

## Character Card Fields

- name
- role in story
- visual identity
- public mask
- private wound
- conscious desire
- hidden need
- fear
- leverage or resource
- constraint
- secret
- lie they believe
- line they will not cross
- first action under pressure
- change arc
- relationship to protagonist
- dialogue style
- visual motifs

## Position Analysis

For every major character, answer:

1. What position do they occupy?
2. What do they control?
3. What do they need from others?
4. Who can stop them?
5. What information do they lack?
6. What would they never say aloud?

This prevents flat heroes and foolish villains.

## Protagonist Test

A protagonist is weak if they only react. Strengthen them by giving:

- a concrete want this episode
- a risky action
- a wrong belief
- a cost for every gain
- a choice that reveals character

## Antagonist Test

A strong antagonist is not merely evil. Give them:

- a rational goal from their own view
- superior resource or information
- a personal weakness
- a line they cross before the protagonist does
- a way to tempt the protagonist

## Relationship Engine

Good relationships contain unequal knowledge. Track:

- what A wants from B
- what B thinks A wants
- what A hides
- what B misreads
- what event forces truth closer

## Dialogue Voice

Give every central character a different speaking rule:

- short commands
- ironic understatement
- over-explaining
- soft threat
- poetic image
- technical precision
- avoidance and silence
- direct emotional naming
