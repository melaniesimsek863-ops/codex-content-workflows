# Story Engine

A strong story is powered by a repeatable engine, not only a premise.

## Core Formula

Use this structure:

- **Protagonist**: who carries the wound, desire, and action.
- **Desire**: what they consciously want now.
- **Wound**: what they cannot admit or have not healed.
- **Constraint**: what makes action difficult.
- **Opponent force**: person, institution, curse, rule, system, family, lover, class, fate, or self-deception.
- **Stakes**: what is lost if they fail.
- **Contradiction**: the desire and wound pull in opposite directions.
- **Escalation machine**: each choice solves one problem and creates a worse one.
- **Ending promise**: what emotional or moral question the story will answer.

## Four Engines

### Desire Engine

Good for romance, career, revenge, competition, and growth stories. Make each episode ask: what does the protagonist want, what blocks them, what price do they pay to move closer?

### Mystery Engine

Good for suspense, rule horror, strange building, campus, family secrets, and puzzle stories. Make each episode reveal one answer and create one deeper question.

### Power Engine

Good for palace, business, school, family, survival, organization, and supernatural hierarchy. Define who has visible power, hidden power, information power, and veto power.

### Emotional Engine

Good for healing, regret, family, relationship, self-worth, and literary short stories. Drive action through unspoken need, delayed understanding, and irreversible choice.

## Stakes Ladder

Escalate from small to large:

1. embarrassment or inconvenience
2. relationship loss
3. status or livelihood loss
4. identity collapse
5. moral compromise
6. physical danger or permanent loss
7. worldview reversal

## Originality Check

A premise is too derivative if the same special setting, protagonist type, opening incident, opponent, twist, and emotional promise all point to a known work. Change at least three deep variables: wound, rule, setting function, opponent logic, ending promise, or moral question.
